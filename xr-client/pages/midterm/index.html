~50 s. Our core problem is latency. Every head turn goes to the robot, and the picture comes back late. [point at the QR] We measure it with a QR code that shows the time: Reachy films it, we read it back. 233 milliseconds, very steady. That's a desktop baseline; the headset number over Wi-Fi is next. Pollen reports about 100, but with hardware encoding on the Wireless version. Our answer is reprojection. [point at the top animation] The naive way: the video is glued to your head, so the room swims. [bottom animation] Ours: each frame is pinned where the robot was looking when it was taken. Your own head turns are instant, only the window edge lags, and the room scan fills the gap.
Problems & blockers

Two blockers, two lessons learned

BLOCKER 1 · FRAME RATE

Video costs half the VR frame rate

72→34fps

Chrome copies every 1080p frame before VR can draw it.

→ frames straight to the GPU (built, testing) · plan B: Unity

BLOCKER 2 · DELAY

Our video is 2× later than Pollen's

233vs~100ms

VP8 encoded in software, 1080p, on the laptop CPU.

→ test 720p and the Mac's hardware H.264 encoder

💻

Robot laptop: Apple silicon only

An Intel Mac was too slow: its 908 ms headset run was invalid.

📶

One Wi-Fi for headset and laptop

Otherwise the video connection never starts.

~35 s. Two real blockers. [left card] First, the browser: copying every 1080p frame into VR halves the frame rate, from 72 to 34, and a low frame rate makes people sick. We now upload frames straight to the GPU; if that's not enough, plan B is Unity. [right card] Second, our delay is twice Pollen's because the video is encoded in software, so we'll test 720p and the Mac's hardware H.264. [bottom] And two lessons: the robot laptop must be Apple silicon, and headset and laptop must share one Wi-Fi.
